## How to choose between them

Start with Google Scholar for breadth, then check CORE for a repository manuscript or DOAJ for open-access journal research. If you already have a specific article, Unpaywall can check for a legal open copy.

Your subject should guide the next step: PMC for complete biomedical articles, ERIC for education, and arXiv for technical fields. Use Semantic Scholar to expand a promising reading list. Preprints belong in your search when early findings matter, with their publication status clearly recorded.

Before citing, check the article’s version, methods, publication status, and any correction or withdrawal notice. Two websites hosting the same study are still one source. Save the article title, DOI, and useful link together; these [free Pocket alternatives for organizing links](/blog/pocket-alternative-free) can help keep a reading queue manageable.

## Quick answers

**What is the best free AI for research?**

Semantic Scholar is a strong starting point for finding papers and screening supported results with short AI summaries. The best AI tools for research still require you to read the underlying studies; a summary does not establish that a conclusion is reliable.

**What are the best websites for MUN research?**

For Model United Nations, use official UN documents and your assigned country’s government statements as primary sources. Google Scholar and DOAJ can add academic context, but a research paper does not establish a country’s official position.

**Can I read research papers for free without a university login?**

Yes. PMC, arXiv, bioRxiv, and PLOS provide freely readable papers, while CORE and DOAJ help locate open research. Coverage varies, and no legitimate service guarantees free access to every published article.
