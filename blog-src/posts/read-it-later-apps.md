## How to choose between them

Start with the device where you actually finish articles. Instapaper is the broadest default for a dedicated queue across Apple and Android devices. PaperSpan is especially relevant when phone reading and offline access matter. Matter fits an Apple-centered routine, while Raindrop.io makes more sense when you mostly need to organize and retrieve links.

For a small backlog, try your browser’s built-in list first. Safari supports deliberate offline saving; Chrome’s desktop list is convenient for clearing tabs, with downloading handled separately. Zotero earns its extra complexity when citations, PDFs, and research notes are part of the job.

Choose wallabag or Karakeep only if you can maintain the server. Open-source software can be free while the infrastructure still needs resources and attention. For a closer comparison of dedicated replacements, see our [free Pocket alternatives](/blog/pocket-alternative-free).

Before committing, save a long article, a page with images, and something from a site you read often. Check formatting and any offline access you need on your actual reading device. A saved URL and a preserved article are different things.

## Quick answers

**Which read it later extension should I use?**

Use Instapaper for a dedicated article queue, Raindrop.io for organized bookmarks, or PaperSpan for a phone-focused reading workflow. Chrome’s desktop Reading List needs no extra extension.

**Which read it later apps android users can use for free?**

Instapaper, PaperSpan, and Raindrop.io offer Android apps with ongoing free core features. Matter does not have an Android app. Start with PaperSpan if offline phone reading is your priority.

**Is a read it later app a free website to read books online?**

No. These tools save articles or organize material you already have access to. Someone searching for a “free website to read books” needs a book catalog or library service; saving a book’s webpage does not provide the book.
