These free Pocket alternatives suit readers who want to save articles, organize useful websites, and find their links again across devices. Instapaper is the closest starting point for article reading; Raindrop.io makes more sense for a mixed collection of articles, tools, videos, and reference pages.

Pocket has closed, so guides that still recommend creating an account or starting a fresh export are outdated. [Mozilla confirmed the shutdown](https://blog.mozilla.org/en/mozilla/building-whats-next/). The options below cover ongoing free use, including one open-source reader that requires your own hosting. Pay attention to the difference between saving a link and keeping an article copy: a bookmark alone cannot protect you when the original page disappears.

## A practical routine for saves you will actually read

Choose one reading inbox. Save three representative pages first: a long article, an image-heavy guide, and something you want offline. Check the reading view and offline behavior on the device you actually use before moving a larger collection.

Keep a small Read next queue and move lasting references into named collections or folders. Once a week, read a few saves, archive useful material, and delete links that no longer interest you. Where an export option exists, keep an occasional backup. ### Quick answers

**Which free Pocket alternative is closest to Pocket?**

Instapaper is the most direct starting point here for saving articles, reading them in a clean view, and returning on another device. Raindrop.io better suits a broad bookmark collection.

**Which Pocket alternative app works on iPhone and Android?**

Instapaper supports both, with free article syncing. Raindrop.io also supports a library across devices. Choose based on whether you mainly read articles or organize links.

**What is a simple Pocket alternative for iOS?**

Safari Reading List is already built in. Matter is another option if you want a separate reading app with tags.

**Is there a free Pocket alternative for Kobo?**

Yes. Instapaper supports reading saved articles on supported Kobo eReaders with a free account. Device compatibility and software support matter, and folders, tags, and notes do not all carry over. Follow [Kobo's setup instructions](https://help.kobo.com/hc/en-us/articles/33359968957463-Use-Instapaper-with-your-Kobo-eReader) and check [Instapaper's Kobo limitations](https://old.instapaper.com/help/kobo) before moving your workflow.
