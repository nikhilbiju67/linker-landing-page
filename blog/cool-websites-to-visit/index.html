12 cool websites to visit when you want something better than scrolling

By the Linkker team · 17 October 2026 · 7 min read

These cool websites to visit suit anyone who wants a quick creative break, a surprising discovery, or something useful to make without paying. You can explore unfamiliar places, turn a rough sketch into a clean drawing, or build a tiny musical loop.

Every pick below offers its main experience for free. Each has a specific moment when it earns a tab, plus the catch to know before you get comfortable.

Quick picks

  1. WindowSwap: A quiet change of scenery
  2. MapCrunch: Accidental travel discoveries
  3. earth: Seeing weather patterns
  4. Chrome Music Lab: Song Maker: Making a first musical loop
  5. Citizen DJ: Finding unusual audio samples
  6. Patatap: Playful sound and animation
  7. AutoDraw: Tidying quick illustrated notes
  8. Quick, Draw!: A quick drawing challenge
  9. LOOPY: Explaining cause and effect
  10. The Password Game: An escalating logic challenge
  11. Zoomquilt: Surreal visual inspiration
  12. NASA's Eyes: Putting space discoveries in context
  1. 1

    WindowSwap

    window-swap.com
    Free plan

    Watch recorded window views submitted by people around the world.

    Open WindowSwap when you have been staring at the same wall all afternoon. Its window videos offer an ordinary slice of somewhere else: passing traffic, moving trees, or a quiet neighborhood. The lack of a sightseeing itinerary is the appeal. These are recorded views, so you can enjoy a change of scenery without expecting live conditions. Basic viewing is free; the full archive and extra navigation features require an upgrade. Visit WindowSwap.

    Best for
    A quiet change of scenery
    The catch
    Videos are prerecorded; archive access, playlists, and extra navigation are restricted.
    Visit window-swap.com ↗
  2. 2

    MapCrunch

    mapcrunch.com
    Free

    Drop into a random Google Street View location and explore from there.

    Reach for MapCrunch when you want to wander without choosing a destination first. Press Go to land somewhere unexpected, then move along the street and look around. Country filters let you narrow the surprise, while Stealth mode hides the location for a guessing challenge. It is a good five-minute geography detour. The free experience depends on available Street View imagery, which may show places as they looked years ago. Explore MapCrunch.

    Best for
    Accidental travel discoveries
    The catch
    Coverage depends on Google Street View, and images may be old.
    Visit mapcrunch.com ↗
  3. 3 E

    earth

    earth.nullschool.net
    Free

    Explore animated maps of global wind, ocean currents, and weather conditions.

    Open earth when a weather map leaves you wondering how the bigger picture fits together. Flowing lines make wind patterns visible across the globe, and the menu switches between atmospheric and ocean layers. Start with surface wind, then compare it with conditions higher up. The map is free to explore. Its movement represents scientific model data updated at intervals, so it takes a little interpretation. Explore earth; the data guide explains the layers.

    Best for
    Seeing weather patterns
    The catch
    The layers take practice to interpret, and the animation represents periodically updated model data.
    Visit earth.nullschool.net ↗
  4. 4 C

    Chrome Music Lab: Song Maker

    musiclab.chromeexperiments.com
    Free

    Build a short song by placing notes and beats on a colorful grid.

    Try Song Maker when you want to make something during a break but have no instrument nearby. Click squares for a melody, add percussion underneath, and hear the pattern loop. It makes the connection between a visual pattern and a musical phrase easy to grasp. Children and adult beginners can start without an account. The controls suit short experiments; complex recording and arrangement need a fuller music tool. Chrome Music Lab explains access and device support.

    Best for
    Making a first musical loop
    The catch
    The tools are basic, and browser compatibility can affect playback.
    Visit musiclab.chromeexperiments.com ↗
  5. 5 C

    Citizen DJ

    citizen-dj.labs.loc.gov
    Free

    Explore Library of Congress sound collections and remix samples in your browser.

    Visit Citizen DJ when a beat needs a sound you would never think to search for. Browse collections of historical music, speech, and found sounds, then combine clips with beats in the browser. Downloadable samples make it useful beyond a quick play session. The project explicitly makes its included sounds free to use for remixing. Expect archival textures and recording noise; those rough edges are part of the material. Explore Citizen DJ.

    Best for
    Finding unusual audio samples
    The catch
    The selection is archival, with variable sound quality; read the accompanying copyright and ethics guidance.
    Visit citizen-dj.labs.loc.gov ↗
  6. 6

    Patatap

    patatap.com
    Free

    Turn keyboard presses or screen taps into sounds and animated shapes.

    Open Patatap when you want an immediate creative response without learning controls first. Each input produces a sound and a moving graphic, letting you improvise little audiovisual patterns. It is especially satisfying for people interested in how timing, color, and sound work together. The browser experience is free to play. Keep your volume modest at first, and pay attention to the site's flashing-image warning before starting. Play Patatap.

    Best for
    Playful sound and animation
    The catch
    Contains flashing images and sudden sounds.
    Visit patatap.com ↗
  7. 7

    AutoDraw

    autodraw.com
    Free

    Match rough doodles with cleaner drawings from an artist-created library.

    Reach for AutoDraw when a slide or illustrated note needs a recognizable object and your sketch is not getting there. Draw a rough outline, inspect the suggestions, and choose a cleaner version. The useful detail is the artist-created library: the tool matches your marks to existing drawings. It is free to use in the browser, with a download option for your work. Unusual subjects may have no useful match. Open AutoDraw.

    Best for
    Tidying quick illustrated notes
    The catch
    Suggestions depend on its existing drawing library, and JavaScript is required.
    Visit autodraw.com ↗
  8. 8

    Quick, Draw!

    quickdraw.withgoogle.com
    Free

    Draw prompted objects while a neural network tries to recognize them.

    Try Quick, Draw! when you want a short game you can explain in one sentence. You have 20 seconds to sketch each prompt while the system guesses aloud. Its mistakes are part of the fun, and the results show how your scribbles compare with other examples. Playing is free. There is a meaningful privacy catch: the project uses contributed drawings in a publicly shared dataset for machine learning research. Read and play Quick, Draw!.

    Best for
    A quick drawing challenge
    The catch
    Drawings contribute to a publicly shared research dataset.
    Visit quickdraw.withgoogle.com ↗
  9. 9

    LOOPY

    ncase.me
    Free & open source

    Draw circles and arrows to make simple interactive models of connected systems.

    Open LOOPY when you are trying to explain a feedback loop, such as how practice builds confidence and confidence encourages practice. Draw the connections, then change a variable and watch the effects travel through the model. It gives students and curious adults a way to test an explanation visually. The tool is free, open source, and public domain. Your model still reflects your assumptions; drawing a connection does not prove it exists. Explore LOOPY.

    Best for
    Explaining cause and effect
    The catch
    Models are simplified illustrations based on the relationships you choose.
    Visit ncase.me ↗
  10. 10

    The Password Game

    neal.fun
    Free

    Solve a puzzle by creating one password that satisfies increasingly awkward rules.

    Choose The Password Game when you want a puzzle that starts familiar and steadily becomes ridiculous. Each new requirement makes you reconsider the string you have already built, turning a routine form field into a juggling act. It is free to open and play, with the challenge appearing directly on the page. Give it more than a spare minute if you want to get far, and use invented text for the puzzle. Play The Password Game.

    Best for
    An escalating logic challenge
    The catch
    Later requirements take patience and may send you looking up clues.
    Visit neal.fun ↗
  11. 11 Z

    Zoomquilt

    zoomquilt.org
    Free

    Watch a collaborative painting zoom endlessly through connected imaginary worlds.

    Visit Zoomquilt when you want a visual reset or an example of an unusual website design. The artwork keeps moving inward, with one illustrated scene opening into the next. Up and down keys let you navigate the zoom. The browser artwork is free to view and rewards close attention to the transitions. It is a continuous motion experience with surreal imagery, so it suits a brief, deliberate visit. View Zoomquilt.

    Best for
    Surreal visual inspiration
    The catch
    Continuous zooming may feel uncomfortable if you are sensitive to motion.
    Visit zoomquilt.org ↗
  12. 12

    NASA's Eyes

    science.nasa.gov
    Free

    Explore planets, asteroids, Earth data, and spacecraft through interactive 3D applications.

    Open NASA's Eyes when a space story makes you want to see where everything actually is. Choose a solar system view, explore an asteroid's approach, or follow a spacecraft through a mission visualization. The free browser applications connect the spectacle to NASA data, giving curious adults and older children plenty to investigate. These are simulations and visualizations, and the interactive 3D views need an internet connection and time to load. Explore NASA's Eyes.

    Best for
    Putting space discoveries in context
    The catch
    Requires an internet connection; the 3D applications can take time to load.
    Visit science.nasa.gov ↗

Keep the sites you will actually revisit

Save each favorite with a note about its job: “make a beat,” “take a quiet break,” or “explain a feedback loop.” That is easier to search later than a folder full of unfamiliar names. For more tools with a learning purpose, browse these useful websites for students.

Quick answers

What are some cool websites for fun?

Start with The Password Game for a challenge, Patatap for instant sound and animation, or MapCrunch for a random virtual walk.

Which cool websites for kids are worth opening together?

Song Maker is a good starting point for making music. AutoDraw offers an approachable drawing activity, while NASA's Eyes gives older children space topics to explore with an adult.

Where can I find cool websites with animation?

Zoomquilt offers continuous illustrated motion, Patatap responds to your input, and earth animates weather patterns. Choose Patatap for interaction or earth when you want the movement to explain something.
