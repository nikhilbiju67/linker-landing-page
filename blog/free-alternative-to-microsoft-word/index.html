8 free alternatives to Microsoft Word for documents, reports, and DOCX

By the Linkker team · 24 October 2026 · 7 min read

This list helps you find a free alternative to Microsoft Word for essays, resumes, reports, and everyday documents. The things you may miss are familiar formatting controls, reliable DOCX files, comments, and working offline. Free tools can cover those jobs, but complicated layouts, Word macros, and specialized add-ins need more care.

Start with LibreOffice for offline writing, Google Docs for shared drafts, or ONLYOFFICE for frequent Word file exchanges. The options below offer ongoing free document editing, rather than a trial that expires. If coursework is your main use, these useful websites for students cover related research and study jobs.

Quick picks

  1. LibreOffice Writer: Offline reports and long documents
  2. Google Docs: Group writing and shared feedback
  3. ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors: Regular DOCX editing on a computer
  4. Zoho Writer: Individual writing with review tools
  5. FreeOffice TextMaker: Letters and everyday desktop documents
  6. Pages: Designed documents on Apple devices
  7. WPS Writer: Familiar Word-style editing
  8. CryptPad: Private collaborative drafts
  1. 1

    LibreOffice Writer

    libreoffice.org
    Free & open source

    Write and format substantial documents offline without creating an account.

    LibreOffice Writer is a strong starting point for reports, essays, and documents you want to keep on your computer. It provides a full desktop word processor within a free office suite, rather than a browser editor with a smaller toolbox. Download it for Windows, macOS, or Linux from the official LibreOffice site.

    It suits people who need sustained offline work and control over local files. The main boundary is Word automation: existing VBA macros generally need adaptation, according to LibreOffice’s compatibility guidance. Check complex Word documents before returning them.

    Best for
    Offline reports and long documents
    The catch
    Complex Word formatting needs checking, and Word macros generally will not run unchanged.
    Visit libreoffice.org ↗
  2. 2

    Google Docs

    workspace.google.com
    Free plan

    Create shared documents that several people can edit and review together.

    Google Docs replaces Word especially well when the document needs several contributors. Shared editing, comments, and version history keep feedback attached to the draft instead of scattered across email attachments. Personal document editing is free with a Google account; Google explains access and features on its Docs product page.

    Use it for group assignments, meeting notes, and reports with straightforward formatting. It can work with Word files, but inspect the exported document when exact pagination matters. Offline editing requires setup beforehand, so open and prepare your files before leaving a connection.

    Best for
    Group writing and shared feedback
    The catch
    A Google account is required; cloud storage is limited, and offline access needs setup.
    Visit workspace.google.com ↗
  3. 3

    ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors

    onlyoffice.com
    Free & open source

    Edit Word documents offline in a desktop suite focused on Office file compatibility.

    ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors is a practical choice when other people keep sending you DOCX files. Its tabbed interface puts multiple documents in one window, and the suite also includes spreadsheet and presentation editors. The free desktop edition works on Windows, macOS, and Linux, including offline work with local files.

    Choose it for routine exchanges with Word users and a familiar document editing workflow. The important distinction is desktop versus hosted collaboration: installing the free app does not provide a managed team cloud. Shared editing needs a connected service or a separate server setup.

    Best for
    Regular DOCX editing on a computer
    The catch
    Requires installation; shared editing depends on a connected cloud service or server.
    Visit onlyoffice.com ↗
  4. 4

    Zoho Writer

    zoho.com
    Free plan

    Write, review, and export documents in a free online word processor for individuals.

    Zoho Writer handles structured documents, comments, collaborative review, and exports to formats including DOCX and PDF. It is useful for people who want a browser editor with substantial document controls. Zoho confirms that ordinary word processing is free on its Writer page.

    A standout detail is its document automation: templates can feed repeated letters and forms. That also introduces a catch, because automated document generation uses a separate credit allowance. Use Writer for individual writing and review, create an account for cloud saving, and check automation limits before planning a bulk workflow.

    Best for
    Individual writing with review tools
    The catch
    Cloud saving requires an account, and automated document generation has a credit allowance.
    Visit zoho.com ↗
  5. 5

    FreeOffice TextMaker

    freeoffice.com
    Free plan

    Create letters and formatted documents with a traditional desktop word processor.

    FreeOffice TextMaker covers everyday letters, reports, tables, and image placement on Windows, macOS, and Linux. It opens and saves both DOC and DOCX, and creates PDFs directly, as its official feature list confirms. That makes it useful when you need editable Word files rather than PDF output alone.

    Choose it if familiar desktop controls matter more than live collaboration. Check specialist requirements first: SoftMaker’s FreeOffice manual identifies omitted tools, including the outliner and thesaurus. Basic document work is free, but the broader TextMaker feature set is not all included.

    Best for
    Letters and everyday desktop documents
    The catch
    Some advanced tools, including the outliner and thesaurus, are excluded from FreeOffice.
    Visit freeoffice.com ↗
  6. 6

    Pages

    support.apple.com
    Free plan

    Create polished documents on Apple devices and export them for Word users.

    Pages is a sensible Word replacement for Mac, iPhone, and iPad users making resumes, illustrated reports, or other documents with careful page design. Apple’s Pages support hub covers creating documents and converting files for sharing.

    The core app remains free. Apple separately reserves premium templates, media, and certain intelligence features for a subscription, as its Creator Studio explanation makes clear. Use the standard tools without starting a trial. For frequent exchanges with Word users, export a DOCX copy and inspect the result; keep your Pages original for further editing.

    Best for
    Designed documents on Apple devices
    The catch
    Some templates and intelligence features require a subscription; Word exports need a layout check.
    Visit support.apple.com ↗
  7. 7

    WPS Writer

    wps.com
    Free plan

    Open, edit, and save DOCX files with a familiar office-style interface.

    WPS Writer covers the core Word workflow: opening a document, changing text and formatting, then saving a DOCX or exporting a PDF. Its official DOCX editor page confirms that basic editing is free. It suits people who want familiar controls across desktop and mobile devices.

    The catch is the surrounding feature mix. PDF conversion and other advanced tools can require an upgrade, while advertising varies by platform. WPS’s advertising guidance distinguishes PC and Android behavior, so do not assume the same experience on every device.

    Best for
    Familiar Word-style editing
    The catch
    Advanced PDF and other tools can require an upgrade; some mobile use includes ads.
    Visit wps.com ↗
  8. 8

    CryptPad

    cryptpad.fr
    Free plan

    Collaborate on encrypted rich text documents in your browser.

    CryptPad replaces Word’s shared drafting role for people who prioritize document privacy. Its browser encryption keeps document content unreadable to the service operator, and the official project offers free accounts on public instances. You can also start a shared document without registering.

    Choose its Rich Text app for notes, collaborative drafts, and simple formatted text. It is a weaker fit for Word file exchanges: the documented import formats are HTML and Markdown, rather than DOCX. Guest documents also have inactivity retention limits, so register and keep exports of important work.

    Best for
    Private collaborative drafts
    The catch
    Rich Text lacks direct DOCX import, storage is limited, and guest documents have inactivity limits.
    Visit cryptpad.fr ↗

Quick answers

What is the best free alternative to Microsoft Word?

LibreOffice Writer is the strongest starting recommendation for offline document work without an account. Google Docs fits shared writing better. ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors deserves a look when exchanging DOCX files is your main task. The right choice depends on the documents you actually need to return to other people.

What is a free alternative to Microsoft Office?

LibreOffice and ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors include document, spreadsheet, and presentation tools. FreeOffice also covers those three jobs. Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides offer a browser-based route for personal use. Replacing the suite takes more checking if your work depends on Excel macros, specialist add-ins, or tightly formatted templates.

Can I use Microsoft Word itself for free?

Yes. Microsoft offers free Word editing in a browser with a Microsoft account. That is useful if you mainly want to avoid paying for desktop Word. It is not a different product provider, and it does not include all desktop capabilities.

Which alternatives work without an account or internet connection?

LibreOffice Writer and ONLYOFFICE Desktop Editors support local offline document work without a cloud account. Browser editors need more preparation: Google Docs and Zoho Writer provide offline options, but enable them while connected rather than assuming an unopened file will be available later.

Will my Word formatting stay exactly the same?

Do not assume it will. Before switching, open a representative document, edit it, save a DOCX copy, and inspect headings, tables, footnotes, fonts, and page breaks. Send a PDF when the recipient only needs to read or print the finished document. Keep an editable original as well.
