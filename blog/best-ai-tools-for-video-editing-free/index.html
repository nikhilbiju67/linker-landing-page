The 8 best free AI tools for video editing

By the Linkker team · 18 October 2026 · 6 min read

These are the best free AI tools for video editing for creators, students, and anyone turning rough footage into something ready to share.

Each qualifies through a usable free editing workflow with AI assistance and no trial timer, with watermarks, account requirements, and usage limits called out below.

Quick picks

  1. Microsoft Clipchamp: Most people editing existing footage
  2. Canva: Captioned social posts and simple explainers
  3. Adobe Express: Quick edits and captions for short clips
  4. Descript: Interviews, podcasts, and spoken tutorials
  5. Shotcut: Desktop editing without cloud transcription quotas
  6. Kdenlive: Longer projects and transcript-assisted assembly
  7. VEED: Short captioned videos where a watermark is acceptable
  8. Kapwing: Occasional short clips and browser-based resizing
  1. 1

    Microsoft Clipchamp

    clipchamp.com
    Free plan

    Edit footage, generate captions, and remove long pauses with free AI assistance.

    Clipchamp is the safest starting point for tutorials, presentations, and everyday social videos. Its personal free plan includes automatic subtitles, AI voiceovers, and silence removal, alongside ordinary trimming and timeline editing. You can export in full HD without a watermark. Microsoft’s plan comparison confirms those features.

    The useful detail is silence removal: it flags longer pauses so you can tighten a recording without hunting through every second. Review its suggestions before accepting them; a pause can be part of the delivery. Choose this if you want several practical AI tools in one approachable editor.

    Best for
    Most people editing existing footage
    The catch
    A personal Microsoft account is required; 4K exports and premium stock assets are excluded.
    Visit clipchamp.com ↗
  2. 2 C

    Canva

    canva.com
    Free plan

    Add AI captions to videos and combine them with simple graphics and layouts.

    Canva suits videos that need readable text, a title card, and a tidy layout more than intricate cutting. Its free auto caption generator transcribes speech, lets you correct the wording, and exports the result as an MP4.

    The standout is keeping captions and visual design together: you can build a short explainer or announcement without moving between separate apps. Canva confirms that free video exports can be watermark-free when you use your own uploads and free assets. Check template contents carefully, since premium elements can introduce a watermark.

    Best for
    Captioned social posts and simple explainers
    The catch
    An account is required, and premium template elements can carry watermarks; other AI features have separate restrictions
    Visit canva.com ↗
  3. 3

    Adobe Express

    adobe.com
    Free plan

    Trim short videos and generate editable captions in a beginner-friendly browser editor.

    Adobe Express works well for a short announcement, class project, or talking-head clip that needs captions and a few clean edits. Its free video editor combines uploaded footage with text, music, and layouts; automatic captioning handles the initial transcription.

    A useful boundary to know before uploading: the Caption video quick action accepts short clips rather than an entire long recording, with file-size, duration, and language requirements. Use it when your main job is making one short video understandable with the sound off. Proofread names and specialized terms before exporting.

    Best for
    Quick edits and captions for short clips
    The catch
    An Adobe account is needed to save your work; captioning has upload limits, and premium assets and advanced AI tools are
    Visit adobe.com ↗
  4. 4

    Descript

    descript.com
    Free plan

    Cut spoken video by editing its automatically generated transcript.

    Descript is especially useful when the problem is what someone said: an interview answer runs long, a tutorial repeats itself, or a recording needs a tighter opening. Its free plan supports transcription and text-based editing, so you can work through spoken material as text instead of locating every cut by ear.

    The important distinction is between that recurring editing allowance and the extra AI tools. Descript’s plan comparison lists monthly media processing but a one-time free AI credit allocation. Choose it for occasional speech-heavy edits, rather than assuming automatic cleanup and generative features renew indefinitely.

    Best for
    Interviews, podcasts, and spoken tutorials
    The catch
    An account is required; media processing renews monthly, but extra AI credits are one-time, and free exports have plan
    Visit descript.com ↗
  5. 5 S

    Shotcut

    shotcut.org
    Free & open source

    Edit videos on your computer and generate subtitles with local speech recognition.

    Shotcut is a strong choice when you want a desktop editor without recurring transcription credits. It is free and open source, runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux, and includes AI speech-to-text subtitle generation through Whisper technology. The project documents its speech model downloader, which handles the model needed for transcription.

    That makes it useful for repeated captioning work after the initial setup. You also get a conventional timeline for cutting footage and adjusting audio. Expect more controls to learn than in Canva, and allow processing time on a slower computer.

    Best for
    Desktop editing without cloud transcription quotas
    The catch
    Requires installation and a speech-model download; transcription speed depends on your computer.
    Visit shotcut.org ↗
  6. 6

    Kdenlive

    kdenlive.org
    Free & open source

    Generate subtitles and assemble spoken clips from transcripts in a full desktop editor.

    Kdenlive is worth learning if you regularly edit interviews, lessons, or longer projects. This free, open-source editor supports speech recognition for both automatic subtitles and transcript-assisted clip selection.

    Its standout is the Speech Editor: you can select passages from a transcript and insert the corresponding footage into the timeline. The speech-to-text documentation explains the workflow and required model configuration. That is useful when building a short answer from a long conversation. Choose it if you want deeper editing control and can spend time setting up the recognition engine.

    Best for
    Longer projects and transcript-assisted assembly
    The catch
    Speech recognition needs model installation and configuration; the editor takes more time to learn than simple browser
    Visit kdenlive.org ↗
  7. 7

    VEED

    veed.io
    Free plan

    Generate and style subtitles while making straightforward edits in your browser.

    VEED fits short captioned clips when a visible watermark is acceptable. Its automatic subtitle workflow is available across plans, subject to allowances, and lets you correct the transcript before finishing the edit.

    The appeal is handling transcription, caption appearance, and basic video adjustments in the same workspace. It is useful for an internal explainer or casual post where speed matters more than a clean branded export. Free exports have a VEED watermark and a 720p resolution ceiling; advanced AI features have separate access limits.

    Best for
    Short captioned videos where a watermark is acceptable
    The catch
    Free exports carry a watermark and are limited to 720p; subtitle allowances and advanced AI access are restricted.
    Visit veed.io ↗
  8. 8 K

    Kapwing

    kapwing.com
    Free plan

    Trim, resize, and automatically subtitle short videos in an online workspace.

    Kapwing is useful for taking a small piece of footage and making it fit a social format, with captions and text added in the browser. Its online editor includes automatic subtitling, while the free plan supports short, watermarked exports.

    The limit to watch is the difference between editing and AI processing: exporting another basic edit does not mean you have unlimited transcription. The free plan comparison lists a small credit allowance for subtitles and text-to-speech. Choose it for occasional short edits where those boundaries and a visible logo are acceptable.

    Best for
    Occasional short clips and browser-based resizing
    The catch
    Free exports have a watermark, reduced resolution, and a short duration cap; uploads and AI credits are also limited.
    Visit kapwing.com ↗

How to choose between them

Start with the part of editing that costs you the most time. Clipchamp covers the broadest everyday workflow: captions, voiceovers, pause removal, and a clean full-HD export. Canva and Adobe Express make more sense when the finished video needs graphics and readable text. Descript suits recordings where you mainly want to cut and rearrange speech.

For regular work without cloud transcription quotas, consider Shotcut or Kdenlive. Their tradeoff is setup and processing time on your computer. VEED and Kapwing are easier to justify for occasional short clips when their watermarks and allowances fit the job.

Before committing to any editor, export a short sample with the exact assets and features you intend to use. Check the resulting resolution, watermark, caption placement, and audio. That catches restrictions before you spend an afternoon on the full project. Always review generated captions and automatic cuts yourself.

Quick answers

What is a good free AI video maker?

For assembling your own clips with AI assistance, Clipchamp is a practical first choice. A free AI video maker that edits existing footage is different from a generator that creates new scenes from prompts; the free editing features listed here do not imply unlimited video generation.

What free video editor app works on Mac?

Shotcut runs on Mac and includes AI subtitle generation after downloading a speech model. It suits people who want an installed editor. Canva also offers a free mobile video editing app, while Clipchamp works on Mac through a supported browser.

How to compress video?

In an editor such as Shotcut, export a copy at a lower resolution or lower video bitrate. Trim unused footage first, then compare the result with your original. Keep the original file: compression can soften detail, and exporting repeatedly can reduce quality further.
