8 free alternatives to Cutlabs for highlights, clips, and Shorts

By the Linkker team · 2 November 2026 · 7 min read

These free alternatives to Cutlabs suit streamers and creators who want to turn existing footage into short clips without a subscription. The hard part to replace is automatic moment selection: finding the good bits inside a long recording, then framing them for a vertical screen. Cutlabs also offers game-aware highlight detection, which is a different job from finding a strong sentence in a podcast.

The first three tools below provide recurring free AI clipping allowances. The remaining five replace the editing and finishing work, with you choosing the highlights. None should be treated as a complete free replacement for every Cutlabs feature. If you already have timestamps and mainly need an editor, our guide to free video editors for PC covers that broader job.

Quick picks

  1. OpusClip: Automatic clip suggestions
  2. Vizard: Editing AI-selected clips
  3. 2short.ai: Captioned YouTube commentary
  4. Microsoft Clipchamp: Simple browser editing
  5. Shotcut: Clipping local recordings
  6. Kdenlive: Organizing repeat clipping work
  7. DaVinci Resolve: Polished picture and sound
  8. OpenShot: Basic manual highlight edits
  1. 1

    OpusClip

    opus.pro
    Free plan

    Automatically turn longer videos into captioned clips with vertical reframing.

    OpusClip is a close starting point if you want software to suggest clips rather than scrub through every minute yourself. Its permanent free plan includes automatic reframing, AI captions, and rendered clips up to 1080p. That replaces a useful chunk of the clipping workflow for interviews, commentary, and other videos built around speech.

    The restriction to notice is editing: the free plan explicitly excludes it. You can get suggested clips, but correcting their structure inside the tool requires more access. Free exports also carry a watermark and have a short download window. Official plan details.

    Best for
    Automatic clip suggestions
    The catch
    Account required, monthly credits, watermarked exports, no free editing, and a short export window.
    Visit opus.pro ↗
  2. 2

    Vizard

    vizard.ai
    Free plan

    Find short clips automatically and adjust them in a browser editor.

    Vizard makes more sense when you expect to revise the clips an AI suggests. Its free plan includes AI-generated clips and full access to the video editor, so it replaces both the initial selection pass and some cleanup afterward. Consider it for webinars, interviews, or recorded explanations where a useful segment needs its opening tightened.

    The trade-off is the finished file: free exports are limited to 720p, and projects have short storage. Its help center also confirms a watermark. Download anything you need promptly. Plan details and free-plan restrictions.

    Best for
    Editing AI-selected clips
    The catch
    Account required, monthly credits, watermarked 720p exports, and short project storage.
    Visit vizard.ai ↗
  3. 3

    2short.ai

    2short.ai
    Free plan

    Extract spoken highlights from captioned YouTube videos and reframe the speaker.

    2short.ai is worth considering when your source is a captioned YouTube video that you own or have permission to repurpose. It uses speech to identify segments, while facial tracking helps keep the active speaker in view. That makes it a better fit for commentary, tutorials, and interviews than gameplay whose best moment happens without much dialogue.

    Its recurring free Starter plan allows limited video analysis and export of generated clips. The distinction matters: exporting an existing suggestion is different from analyzing another long upload. It also requires source videos with captions. Official features and free-plan details.

    Best for
    Captioned YouTube commentary
    The catch
    Account required, limited monthly analysis, and captioned source videos required; some import options are restricted.
    Visit 2short.ai ↗
  4. 4

    Microsoft Clipchamp

    clipchamp.com
    Free plan

    Trim, crop, and assemble short videos in a browser with free watermark-free exports.

    Clipchamp replaces the straightforward editing stage after you have picked a moment. Bring in your recording, trim the section, crop it for the intended screen, and add text where it helps. Its free plan includes those basic tools and watermark-free exports, making it useful when an AI service’s branding is the main obstacle.

    It is a practical choice for occasional clips and creators who prefer a browser editor. It does not replace game-aware highlight discovery across an entire stream. A personal Microsoft account is required, and premium assets and export options sit outside the free offering. Official comparison.

    Best for
    Simple browser editing
    The catch
    Microsoft account required; premium assets and some export options are excluded.
    Visit clipchamp.com ↗
  5. 5 S

    Shotcut

    shotcut.org
    Free & open source

    Cut local recordings into finished clips with a free desktop video editor.

    Shotcut is useful when you have the recording on your computer and want to control the edit yourself. Its broad format support reduces the need to convert files before starting, and its in-and-out controls let you mark a section precisely. It replaces manual trimming and assembly without a cloud processing allowance.

    Choose it for repeat clipping from local recordings, especially if uploading large files is inconvenient. The cost is your time: you still need to locate the moments and build the presentation. Large recordings can also require proxy files for smoother preview playback. Official overview and system requirements.

    Best for
    Clipping local recordings
    The catch
    Desktop installation and manual highlight selection required; large files can strain slower computers.
    Visit shotcut.org ↗
  6. 6

    Kdenlive

    kdenlive.org
    Free & open source

    Build short edits from longer recordings using tracks, markers, and organized media bins.

    Kdenlive fits creators who want a repeatable editing setup for streams or episodes. Markers help you keep track of moments, while bins organize the recordings, graphics, and audio used across clips. Its proxy workflow is particularly useful when the original files are too heavy for comfortable timeline playback.

    It replaces the editing workspace rather than Cutlabs’ automatic discovery. You decide where each highlight starts, what needs cutting, and how the final sequence flows. Use it when you make several clips from each recording and want room to organize that work. Official features and supported platforms.

    Best for
    Organizing repeat clipping work
    The catch
    Desktop installation, a learning curve, and manual decisions about which moments to clip.
    Visit kdenlive.org ↗
  7. 7 D

    DaVinci Resolve

    blackmagicdesign.com
    Free plan

    Edit and finish clips with detailed control over picture, sound, and pacing.

    DaVinci Resolve is the option for clips that need more finishing than an automatic crop and captions. Its free desktop version combines editing, color correction, visual effects, and audio tools. That gives you space to repair uneven sound or match footage before assembling a polished highlight.

    It replaces the finishing side of Cutlabs, with a much larger learning commitment. Do not assume demonstrations of Studio’s AI features apply to the free download; Blackmagic separates those editions. Choose Resolve if editing quality matters enough to justify learning a full production application. Official edition comparison.

    Best for
    Polished picture and sound
    The catch
    Demanding desktop software with a learning curve; Studio-only AI features and format support are excluded.
    Visit blackmagicdesign.com ↗
  8. 8

    OpenShot

    openshot.org
    Free & open source

    Trim and arrange selected highlights in a free desktop timeline.

    OpenShot suits the smaller job: take a known section of footage, remove the dead space, add a title, and export it. Its timeline supports multiple video and audio tracks, with frame-accurate trimming and controls for positioning and scaling footage. The official site explicitly offers editing without watermarks or project limits.

    That makes it useful for occasional highlights when a cloud clip maker’s allowance is unnecessary. It leaves the important selection work to you, though. Expect to review your own recording and adjust framing manually rather than ask it to recognize a decisive gameplay event. Official features.

    Best for
    Basic manual highlight edits
    The catch
    Desktop installation required; highlight selection and framing take manual work.
    Visit openshot.org ↗

Quick answers

Which Cutlabs free alternative is closest to automatic clipping?

OpusClip and Vizard are the closest options here for getting suggested clips from longer footage. Vizard includes editor access on its free plan; OpusClip’s free plan excludes editing. Both apply watermarks and processing limits.

Which option replaces automatic gaming highlights?

None of these is a complete replacement for game-aware event detection. Speech-focused clippers may find a strong reaction or explanation, but that does not mean they understand the play itself. For gameplay, recording timestamps as you go makes a manual editor much more useful.

Can I make clips without a watermark for free?

Yes. Clipchamp offers free watermark-free exports when you stay within its free features. OpenShot also explicitly offers editing without watermarks. Both require you to choose and edit the highlight yourself.

Can I process a long stream without monthly AI credits?

A local editor such as Shotcut or Kdenlive avoids a cloud AI processing allowance. Your computer’s storage and performance become the practical limits, and you supply the time needed to review the recording.

What should I check before uploading a whole recording?

Check the source-length allowance, export restrictions, watermark, and storage window. Start with a short recording you own, then export a finished clip before committing a lengthy stream to the workflow.
