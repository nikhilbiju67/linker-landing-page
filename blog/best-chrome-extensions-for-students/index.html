11 best Chrome extensions for students, from research to deadlines

By the Linkker team · 25 October 2026 · 7 min read

The best Chrome extensions for students help you finish actual coursework: capture an assignment, find a readable paper, keep its citation, and submit a cleaner draft. This starter kit suits high school and college students using Chrome on a computer, with free tools arranged around a typical study week.

Every pick has a useful free core without a trial timer. You don’t need all 12; start with the job that keeps slowing you down. For tools you can use directly in a browser, pair this kit with these useful websites for students.

Quick picks

  1. Todoist: Capturing assignments and planning the week
  2. LeechBlock NG: Keeping study sessions focused
  3. OneTab: Parking tabs between study sessions
  4. Unpaywall: Finding accessible research papers
  5. Zotero Connector: Essays, lab reports, and bibliographies
  6. SingleFile: Offline reading and preserving webpages
  7. Google Translate: Understanding unfamiliar words and passages
  8. Read Aloud: Listening to readings and reviewing drafts
  9. Dark Reader: Adjusting website appearance for reading
  10. LanguageTool: Proofreading coursework and emails
  11. Bitwarden: Managing school and personal logins
  1. 1

    Todoist

    todoist.com
    Free plan

    Turn assignment pages into tasks you can schedule and check off.

    Start the week by turning scattered course pages into a short task list. Todoist’s Chrome extension lets you capture tasks while browsing, so an assignment link can sit beside its due date instead of disappearing into another tab. Use clear actions such as “outline history essay” rather than just “history.” The free Beginner plan covers everyday tasks and reminders, though personal projects and activity history are limited.

    Best for
    Capturing assignments and planning the week
    The catch
    An account is required, and the free plan limits personal projects, filters, and activity history.
    Visit todoist.com ↗
  2. 2 L

    LeechBlock NG

    proginosko.com
    Free

    Block distracting websites during the study periods you choose.

    Give your first study session a defined boundary. LeechBlock NG can block selected sites on a schedule, after a browsing allowance, or immediately through its lockdown feature. Separate block sets let you treat social feeds differently from sites you occasionally need for class. The developer describes it as a free productivity tool. It suits students who know exactly where their study time goes, but need help interrupting the habit.

    Best for
    Keeping study sessions focused
    The catch
    You must configure the sites and rules yourself; it only controls browsing where the extension is installed.
    Visit proginosko.com ↗
  3. 3 O

    OneTab

    one-tab.com
    Free

    Collapse open tabs into a list and restore them when you need them.

    When three subjects have taken over your tab bar, OneTab gives you room to work. Its core tab collection works without registration, and you can reopen individual pages instead of restoring everything. Use it to park background reading while keeping the current assignment visible. Remember that it stores links, so reopening a page still depends on that website. The official help page also warns that uninstalling deletes stored tabs; export important lists.

    Best for
    Parking tabs between study sessions
    The catch
    Saved links do not preserve page contents, and uninstalling the extension deletes locally stored tabs.
    Visit one-tab.com ↗
  4. 4 U

    Unpaywall

    unpaywall.org
    Free

    Find legally available free copies of scholarly articles.

    Before abandoning a paper at a publisher’s access screen, let Unpaywall check for an open copy. Its official extension listing explains that it links to authorized full text, including manuscripts hosted by universities and government sites. A green tab appears when it finds a match. This is especially useful for literature reviews, but coverage depends on an accessible copy existing; your library remains useful when nothing turns up.

    Best for
    Finding accessible research papers
    The catch
    It cannot find a free copy of every paper, and the available manuscript may differ from the publisher’s formatted PDF.
    Visit unpaywall.org ↗
  5. 5

    Zotero Connector

    zotero.org
    Free & open source

    Save research sources and their bibliographic details into Zotero.

    Save the citation when you find the source, while the author and publication details are still in front of you. Zotero Connector recognizes supported research pages and can save available PDFs alongside their records. The official listing confirms that Zotero is free and open source. Install the desktop app for organizing references and inserting citations into documents. Always check imported metadata before submitting a bibliography.

    Best for
    Essays, lab reports, and bibliographies
    The catch
    The full workflow needs Zotero’s desktop app; free cloud storage for attachments is limited.
    Visit zotero.org ↗
  6. 6

    SingleFile

    github.com
    Free & open source

    Save a webpage as one HTML file for later reading.

    Keep an offline copy of an important explainer or reference page before heading somewhere with unreliable Wi-Fi. SingleFile packages a page into one HTML file that you can open later in a browser. Its official repository also documents highlighting, notes, and saving selected content. It is useful for preserving reading material, but treat the result as a snapshot: interactive tools and live information need the original website.

    Best for
    Offline reading and preserving webpages
    The catch
    Saved snapshots do not reproduce every interactive feature; check the file before relying on it offline.
    Visit github.com ↗
  7. 7

    Google Translate

    chromewebstore.google.com
    Free

    Translate selected passages while you read websites.

    Use Google Translate when one unfamiliar passage interrupts an otherwise readable source. Highlight or right-click text to see a translation without copying it into another tab. The Google-published listing makes an important distinction: the extension handles selected text, while whole-page translation is built into Chrome. It helps with understanding foreign-language reading, though technical terms and quotations still deserve a dictionary or another reliable check.

    Best for
    Understanding unfamiliar words and passages
    The catch
    Whole-page translation uses Chrome’s built-in feature, and machine translations can miss context.
    Visit chromewebstore.google.com ↗
  8. 8 R

    Read Aloud

    readaloud.app
    Free & open source

    Read webpage text aloud using text-to-speech voices.

    Switch to listening when another screenful of reading feels difficult, or hear a draft to catch awkward sentences. Read Aloud can speak a selected passage, which is handy when navigation menus clutter a page. Choose a native browser voice for the free core workflow. Its official store listing distinguishes those voices from optional cloud voices that may require additional access. Voice quality varies, especially with specialist vocabulary.

    Best for
    Listening to readings and reviewing drafts
    The catch
    Some cloud voices require separate access or an API key; native voice quality varies by device.
    Visit readaloud.app ↗
  9. 9

    Dark Reader

    darkreader.org
    Free & open source

    Apply adjustable dark colors to bright websites.

    Make a bright reading page more comfortable to look at without changing your entire computer’s appearance. Dark Reader offers brightness, contrast, and sepia controls, plus settings for individual websites. Its official Chrome listing describes automatic theme generation and site exclusions. It suits students who prefer darker pages during long reading sessions. Disable it when inspecting artwork, charts, or anything whose original colors matter.

    Best for
    Adjusting website appearance for reading
    The catch
    Some pages need a site exception, and other extensions that change page colors can conflict with it.
    Visit darkreader.org ↗
  10. 10

    LanguageTool

    languagetool.org
    Free plan

    Flag spelling, grammar, and punctuation problems as you write online.

    Run a final language check on discussion posts, emails, and assignment drafts. LanguageTool supports multiple languages and offers basic corrections through its browser add-on. Its free Basic option includes everyday grammar and spelling checks, with limits on text length and advanced suggestions. Set the appropriate English variant before reviewing a paper. Accept edits selectively, particularly around subject terminology, quotations, and sentences whose meaning a suggested change could alter.

    Best for
    Proofreading coursework and emails
    The catch
    Free checking has text-length limits, and advanced grammar and style suggestions are restricted.
    Visit languagetool.org ↗
  11. 11

    Bitwarden

    bitwarden.com
    Free plan

    Generate, store, and autofill passwords for your school and personal accounts.

    Finish setting up your study browser by sorting out its logins. Bitwarden’s free personal plan includes unlimited passwords and devices, plus password generation and autofill through its Chrome extension. The official feature comparison confirms those core features remain free. It suits students moving between a laptop and phone who want separate passwords for each service. Create the account carefully and keep your master password somewhere secure.

    Best for
    Managing school and personal logins
    The catch
    An account is required, and you must protect your master password; some additional security features are restricted.
    Visit bitwarden.com ↗

Set it up in 10 minutes

  1. Minutes 1–2: Choose one immediate problem: missed tasks, distracting sites, scattered sources, or proofreading. Install the matching extension from its official page.
  2. Minutes 3–5: Configure one useful action. Add an assignment with a due date, create a study block, or save a research source.
  3. Minutes 6–8: Try that action on real coursework. Check the saved citation, reopen the offline page, or listen to a paragraph.
  4. Minutes 9–10: Pin the extension you will use regularly. Add another only when you can name its job. Zotero’s desktop setup may need extra time.

Quick answers

Best Chrome extensions for Android

Chrome on Android phones does not run these desktop extensions. “Add to Desktop” queues an extension for your computer. Use mobile apps where available, such as Todoist or Bitwarden. Google’s installation guidance explains the distinction.

Best Chrome extensions for YouTube

Video Speed Controller is the relevant pick here for reviewing tutorials. If YouTube’s built-in playback controls already cover your needs, you can skip the extension.

Best Chrome extensions for teachers

Zotero Connector helps collect references for lessons, LanguageTool checks written instructions, and Read Aloud provides a listening option. On school-managed computers, administrators may restrict installation, as Google’s help page explains.

Best Chrome extensions for work

Todoist, Bitwarden, and LanguageTool also fit internships and part-time jobs: track tasks, manage logins, and review emails. Follow your workplace’s rules before installing them.
