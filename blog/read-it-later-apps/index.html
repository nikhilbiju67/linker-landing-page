The 9 best free read it later apps, with every catch explained

By the Linkker team · 10 November 2026 · 7 min read

These are the best free read it later apps for people who want to save articles on a computer and actually finish them on a phone, tablet, or browser.

Each qualifies through ongoing free article saving, a useful built-in reading list, or open-source software you can run on your own hardware; options appear in order from broad everyday usefulness to specialist setups.

Quick picks

  1. Instapaper: Everyday article reading across different devices
  2. Raindrop.io: A reading queue inside a larger bookmark library
  3. PaperSpan: Phone reading during commutes or patchy connections
  4. Matter: Dedicated article reading on iPhone and iPad
  5. Safari: A simple reading list on Apple devices
  6. Google Chrome: Clearing unread tabs on a computer
  7. Zotero: Students, researchers, and reading that needs citations
  8. wallabag: Readers comfortable maintaining their own article archive
  9. Karakeep: Technical users collecting articles and mixed reference material
  1. 1 I

    Instapaper

    instapaper.com
    Free plan

    Save web articles into a simple reading queue that syncs across the web, iPhone, and Android.

    Instapaper is the safest starting point if your main goal is to collect articles and read them without a busy website around the text. Its free account includes unlimited saves, folders, and syncing across web, iOS, and Android. That makes it practical when your work computer and personal phone use different platforms.

    The important distinction is saving versus preserving: a permanent archive is a Premium feature. Full-text search, the PDF reader, Kindle integration, and enhanced listening tools also sit outside the free core. Check the official feature comparison.

    Best for
    Everyday article reading across different devices
    The catch
    Requires an account; the free website has ads, notes are limited, and permanent archiving and several reading extras are
    Visit instapaper.com ↗
  2. 2

    Raindrop.io

    raindrop.io
    Free plan

    Organize articles and other saved links into searchable collections with tags and highlights.

    Raindrop.io suits readers whose backlog includes tutorials, recipes, reference pages, and videos alongside long articles. Its free plan supports unlimited bookmarks, collections, highlights, and devices, with apps and extensions across major platforms. A dedicated “Read next” collection is an easy way to separate unfinished reading from permanent reference material.

    Choose it when organizing links matters as much as reading them. Free bookmarking does not include the web archive or searching the complete text of saved pages, so it should not be treated as a permanent article backup. See the free-plan details.

    Best for
    A reading queue inside a larger bookmark library
    The catch
    Requires sign-up; permanent page copies, full-text search, reminders, and duplicate detection are excluded.
    Visit raindrop.io ↗
  3. 3

    PaperSpan

    paperspan.com
    Free plan

    Save articles for clean reading on the web and offline reading on your phone.

    PaperSpan is a useful choice for people who collect articles at a desk and read them somewhere with unreliable reception. Its official site offers free apps for Android, iPhone, iPad, and the web, while browser extensions send articles into your library. The service also describes itself as having no ads or tracking.

    Pay attention to the platform differences. The mobile apps support offline reading and text-to-speech, but the web app does not offer those features. Pick PaperSpan primarily for phone reading rather than expecting identical tools everywhere. Compare its apps before choosing.

    Best for
    Phone reading during commutes or patchy connections
    The catch
    Requires an account; offline reading and text-to-speech are unavailable in the web app.
    Visit paperspan.com ↗
  4. 4

    Matter

    getmatter.com
    Free plan

    Build an uncapped article library for reading on iPhone, iPad, and the web.

    Matter fits readers who want a dedicated reading app and mainly use Apple mobile devices. Its free service includes an uncapped library, saving through mobile and browser extensions, readable article formatting, and unlimited tags. You can keep a substantial queue without a trial deadline forcing a decision.

    Separate that basic queue from the features highlighted elsewhere on the homepage. Listening, the newsletter inbox, and advanced annotation tools belong to Premium. There is also no Android app, which makes Matter a weaker choice for mixed-phone households. Read Matter’s free-service and platform answers.

    Best for
    Dedicated article reading on iPhone and iPad
    The catch
    Requires sign-up and has no Android app; listening, newsletter delivery, and advanced annotation features are extra.
    Visit getmatter.com ↗
  5. 5

    Safari

    support.apple.com
    Free

    Keep webpages in Safari’s built-in Reading List and save selected pages for offline reading.

    Safari’s Reading List is a sensible first stop if you already browse on a Mac, iPhone, or iPad. There is no separate reading service to join, and adding a page takes only a trip through the Share menu. On a Mac, you can also Shift-click a link to put it in the list without opening another tab.

    Offline saving deserves a deliberate check: use Save Offline for individual pages or enable automatic saving. Choose this for a straightforward personal queue rather than a heavily categorized research library. Apple explains the controls and offline settings.

    Best for
    A simple reading list on Apple devices
    The catch
    Tied to Safari on Apple devices; offline copies require saving or the automatic offline setting.
    Visit support.apple.com ↗
  6. 6

    Google Chrome

    support.google.com
    Free

    Put desktop tabs into a built-in Reading List without installing another extension.

    Chrome’s desktop Reading List works well when your immediate problem is a row of tabs you cannot read right now. Right-click a tab and choose Add tab to reading list, then return to it from the browser’s Reading List panel. You can reorder, edit, and delete entries there.

    The useful catch is that adding a link does not automatically make it an offline article. Google documents Save page as a separate step for offline access on a computer. Choose Chrome for lightweight browser housekeeping, especially when you do not need another dedicated app. See Google’s instructions.

    Best for
    Clearing unread tabs on a computer
    The catch
    The desktop Reading List saves links; offline reading requires separately downloading the page.
    Visit support.google.com ↗
  7. 7

    Zotero

    zotero.org
    Free & open source

    Save research articles, PDFs, and webpage snapshots alongside notes and citation details.

    Zotero is the better fit when “read later” also means “cite this in a report.” Its browser Connector captures source information, and its desktop library lets you organize material into collections with tags and notes. It can save accessible PDFs and webpage snapshots, giving your reading queue more substance than a folder of URLs.

    The software works locally for free, and syncing library data is free and unlimited. Attached files use a separate, limited storage allowance. Expect more setup and library management than a casual article reader. Check the distinction between data and file syncing.

    Best for
    Students, researchers, and reading that needs citations
    The catch
    Cloud storage for PDFs and snapshots is limited; the desktop workflow uses an app plus a browser Connector.
    Visit zotero.org ↗
  8. 8

    wallabag

    wallabag.org
    Free & open source

    Extract articles into a personal reading archive hosted on your own server.

    wallabag makes sense when you want control over the saved article itself. It extracts page content into a clean reading view and supports an ecosystem of browser extensions, mobile apps, RSS readers, and e-readers. That is a useful foundation for a reading library you intend to keep and maintain yourself.

    The free option here is the open-source software running on hardware you already control. The official managed service has a trial rather than an ongoing free account. Use wallabag if server installation, backups, and updates are tasks you can comfortably handle. Compare its hosting options.

    Best for
    Readers comfortable maintaining their own article archive
    The catch
    Requires your own server and maintenance to use free; the managed service is only a trial.
    Visit wallabag.org ↗
  9. 9 K

    Karakeep

    karakeep.com
    Free & open source

    Keep articles, notes, images, and PDFs in a self-hosted library with a clean reader view.

    Karakeep suits technically confident readers who want one library for more than articles. It combines bookmarking with full-page archives, full-text search, reader mode, and highlighting. Its official site also lists mobile apps and browser extensions, so collecting material need not happen only at the server’s computer.

    The qualifying free option is self-hosting the open-source software with Docker. Treat the advertised cloud trial separately. Start by assessing its basic saving and reading workflow before adding optional automation; you are responsible for the installation and the resources it uses. See the self-hostable project and reading features.

    Best for
    Technical users collecting articles and mixed reference material
    The catch
    Free use requires self-hosting and ongoing maintenance; the cloud offer is a trial.
    Visit karakeep.com ↗

How to choose between them

Start with the device where you actually finish articles. Instapaper is the broadest default for a dedicated queue across Apple and Android devices. PaperSpan is especially relevant when phone reading and offline access matter. Matter fits an Apple-centered routine, while Raindrop.io makes more sense when you mostly need to organize and retrieve links.

For a small backlog, try your browser’s built-in list first. Safari supports deliberate offline saving; Chrome’s desktop list is convenient for clearing tabs, with downloading handled separately. Zotero earns its extra complexity when citations, PDFs, and research notes are part of the job.

Choose wallabag or Karakeep only if you can maintain the server. Open-source software can be free while the infrastructure still needs resources and attention. For a closer comparison of dedicated replacements, see our free Pocket alternatives.

Before committing, save a long article, a page with images, and something from a site you read often. Check formatting and any offline access you need on your actual reading device. A saved URL and a preserved article are different things.

Quick answers

Which read it later extension should I use?

Use Instapaper for a dedicated article queue, Raindrop.io for organized bookmarks, or PaperSpan for a phone-focused reading workflow. Chrome’s desktop Reading List needs no extra extension.

Which read it later apps android users can use for free?

Instapaper, PaperSpan, and Raindrop.io offer Android apps with ongoing free core features. Matter does not have an Android app. Start with PaperSpan if offline phone reading is your priority.

Is a read it later app a free website to read books online?

No. These tools save articles or organize material you already have access to. Someone searching for a “free website to read books” needs a book catalog or library service; saving a book’s webpage does not provide the book.
