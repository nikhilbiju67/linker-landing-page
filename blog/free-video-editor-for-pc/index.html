The 10 best free video editors for PC

By the Linkker team · 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

This list is for anyone searching for a free video editor for PC to cut home movies, make YouTube videos, or finish social clips on Windows.

Each pick offers ongoing free editing and usable exports without a trial timer; the order favors broad usefulness, with specialized tools further down.

Quick picks

  1. Shotcut: Everyday desktop editing without an account
  2. Clipchamp: Beginners making HD videos
  3. OpenShot: Simple montages and first timeline projects
  4. DaVinci Resolve: Ambitious projects and detailed color work
  5. Kdenlive: Longer projects with several tracks
  6. Canva: Template-based social and presentation videos
  7. Adobe Express: Short branded announcements and explainers
  8. VSDC Free Video Editor: Personal and educational Windows projects
  9. LosslessCut: Quick cuts that preserve original quality
  10. Blender: Videos that include Blender animation
  1. 1 S

    Shotcut

    shotcut.org
    Free & open source

    Edit video, audio, and titles on your PC without a subscription or account.

    Shotcut is the safest starting point for most readers who want a full desktop editor without managing a free plan. It supports a wide range of formats and lets you combine footage with different resolutions and frame rates in one project. Its movable panels also let you simplify the workspace as you learn.

    Choose it for regular YouTube videos, home movies, and projects built from your own footage. Expect to learn timeline editing and export settings rather than follow a template. See the official Shotcut overview.

    Best for
    Everyday desktop editing without an account
    The catch
    The interface takes practice, and demanding footage can require proxy files for comfortable editing.
    Visit shotcut.org ↗
  2. 2

    Clipchamp

    clipchamp.com
    Free plan

    Trim clips, add captions, and export clean HD videos through a browser or Windows app.

    Clipchamp suits people who want to finish a straightforward video without learning a large production suite. The personal free plan covers core editing and watermark-free exports up to 1080p. Screen recording, text, transitions, and caption tools make it useful for presentations and short tutorials.

    The restriction to watch is premium content: adding a premium asset or feature can block export until you remove it or change plans. Start with your own footage and free assets. Microsoft's Clipchamp explanation confirms the free export allowance.

    Best for
    Beginners making HD videos
    The catch
    An account is required; 4K exports and premium assets are outside the personal free plan.
    Visit clipchamp.com ↗
  3. 3

    OpenShot

    openshot.org
    Free & open source

    Arrange clips, photos, music, and titles in a conventional desktop timeline.

    OpenShot is a sensible choice for photo montages, classroom projects, and family videos. You can stack video and audio tracks, trim clips, add transitions, and animate properties such as position or volume. There are no subscription gates, watermarks, or project limits.

    Its title templates help when you need an opening card or credits without designing everything yourself. Download the free Windows EXE from the official site; the optional Microsoft Store listing is a separate distribution choice. The official download page explains that distinction.

    Best for
    Simple montages and first timeline projects
    The catch
    Animated 3D titles require Blender, and complex projects still involve learning tracks and keyframes.
    Visit openshot.org ↗
  4. 4 D

    DaVinci Resolve

    blackmagicdesign.com
    Free plan

    Edit, color-grade, mix audio, and build effects inside one desktop application.

    DaVinci Resolve is the pick for readers who expect their editing needs to grow. Its free edition combines editing, color tools, Fusion effects, and Fairlight audio work, so you can finish much more than a basic sequence without switching applications.

    Blackmagic specifies support for virtually all 8-bit formats up to Ultra HD at 60fps in the free edition. Check compatibility before committing to unusual camera footage. Choose it for substantial YouTube projects or short films, with time reserved for learning. The official comparison separates free features from Studio.

    Best for
    Ambitious projects and detailed color work
    The catch
    It demands capable hardware and substantial learning; advanced AI tools and some format support require Studio.
    Visit blackmagicdesign.com ↗
  5. 5

    Kdenlive

    kdenlive.org
    Free & open source

    Build detailed multitrack videos with proxy editing and project organization tools.

    Kdenlive fits editors who want room for longer projects without premium feature badges. It offers nested sequences, markers, media bins, and automatic backups, which become useful when a project grows beyond a handful of clips.

    Its standout practical feature is proxy editing: work with lighter copies of demanding footage while retaining the originals for the finished export. That can help on a modest PC, though generating proxies takes time and storage. Choose it for recurring tutorials, interviews, and organized multitrack work. See Kdenlive's feature overview.

    Best for
    Longer projects with several tracks
    The catch
    The workspace has a learning curve, and proxy files need extra preparation time and disk space.
    Visit kdenlive.org ↗
  6. 6 C

    Canva

    canva.com
    Free plan

    Turn clips, photos, and text into videos using ready-made layouts.

    Canva is useful when the main job is arranging words and visuals for a short announcement, lesson, or social post. Its templates give you a starting layout instead of an empty timeline, and you can combine uploaded footage with free design elements.

    Free accounts can export without a watermark when every element is free. Pro content changes that, so check the assets throughout the project before finishing. Choose Canva when readable text and consistent visual design matter more than precise audio editing. Its video editor FAQ explains the watermark rule.

    Best for
    Template-based social and presentation videos
    The catch
    An account is required, and Pro elements introduce watermarks unless removed or licensed.
    Visit canva.com ↗
  7. 7

    Adobe Express

    adobe.com
    Free plan

    Assemble short videos with trimming, text, music, and design templates.

    Adobe Express suits quick announcements, explainers, and social videos where you want guidance from a template. Its free browser editor handles the core work of arranging and trimming footage, adding text, and combining visuals with audio.

    Use your own media or clearly marked free assets to keep the project exportable. Premium content can prevent a free account from downloading the result, including after complimentary Premium access ends. Choose it for short design-led projects rather than intricate productions. Read the editor overview and premium-content restrictions.

    Best for
    Short branded announcements and explainers
    The catch
    An account is required; premium assets and features are restricted, and premium content can block downloads.
    Visit adobe.com ↗
  8. 8

    VSDC Free Video Editor

    videosoftdev.com
    Free plan

    Create layered Windows videos with effects, titles, and watermark-free exports.

    VSDC offers cutting, music, transitions, titles, and standard effects without adding a watermark or export time limit. It also supports high-resolution exports, making it worth considering for personal projects that need more than basic trimming.

    The licensing catch matters: its official FAQ limits the free edition to personal and educational use and requires an appropriate Pro license for commercial work. Hardware-accelerated export and several advanced tools are also Pro features. Choose it for noncommercial Windows projects after reviewing the official Free and Pro FAQ.

    Best for
    Personal and educational Windows projects
    The catch
    Commercial work requires an appropriate license; hardware-accelerated export and advanced tools require Pro.
    Visit videosoftdev.com ↗
  9. 9

    LosslessCut

    losslesscut.app
    Free & open source

    Remove unwanted sections from existing videos without re-encoding the whole file.

    LosslessCut solves a narrower job than the editors above: keep the useful parts of a recording and discard the rest. It copies the retained video data rather than rendering every frame again, preserving the original quality for supported operations.

    That makes it useful for trimming long recordings or extracting sections before doing detailed editing elsewhere. The trade-off is precision: lossless cuts depend on keyframes and may start earlier than your selected point. It is not a replacement for titles and layered effects. See the official features and limitations.

    Best for
    Quick cuts that preserve original quality
    The catch
    Lossless cut points depend on keyframes, and available operations vary by file format and codec.
    Visit losslesscut.app ↗
  10. 10

    Blender

    blender.org
    Free & open source

    Combine video editing with animation and compositing in a free desktop suite.

    Blender makes sense if your video also involves animation or effects you are creating in Blender. Its Video Sequencer can arrange clips, synchronize audio, add transitions, and work with multiple channels, while the wider application handles animation and compositing.

    For a basic holiday montage, that is a lot of software to learn. For someone already creating animated scenes, it can keep more of the production in one application. Start in the Video Editing workspace. The official sequencer manual explains how clips, effects, and audio fit together.

    Best for
    Videos that include Blender animation
    The catch
    The wider 3D interface adds a steep learning curve for people who only need ordinary video cuts.
    Visit blender.org ↗

How to choose a free video editor for PC

Start with Shotcut if you want a general desktop editor without an account. Choose Clipchamp for a simpler route to a finished HD video, or OpenShot for straightforward montages. DaVinci Resolve and Kdenlive suit projects that need more detailed control.

For text-heavy social posts, Canva or Adobe Express can save setup time. Pick LosslessCut when removing sections is the entire job. VSDC's commercial-use restriction deserves attention before using it for client work.

Before building a long project, import a representative clip and export a short section. Check picture quality, sound, captions, watermark behavior, and whether your chosen effects are free. This also reveals format problems before you invest hours.

Quick answers

Which free video editor for PC has no watermark?

Shotcut, OpenShot, and Kdenlive offer free desktop editing without an export watermark. Clipchamp's personal free plan also exports without a watermark up to 1080p when you stay within its included features.

Is there a free video editor AI option?

Clipchamp includes AI-assisted tools such as automatic captions and auto composition. Those help edit existing material. Check individual feature availability before building a project around one AI tool.

Is a free AI video maker the same as a video editor?

Usually, a generator creates footage from prompts or images, while an editor arranges footage you already have. If you need generated clips, see these free AI video generators and their catches.
