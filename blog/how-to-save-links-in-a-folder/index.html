6 free tools for how to save links in a folder

By the Linkker team · 11 November 2026 · 5 min read

These six free tools show you how to save links in a folder, whether you want a simple browser bookmark folder or a collection you can open on your phone and computer. The quickest method: create a folder in your browser’s bookmark manager, bookmark a page, and choose that folder as its location.

Quick picks

  1. Google Chrome: Everyday bookmarks in Chrome
  2. Safari: iPhone and Mac users
  3. Firefox: A manually organized reference library
  4. Raindrop.io: Collections across browsers and devices
  5. Start.me: A visual dashboard of regular links
  6. Google Drive: Links alongside project documents
  1. 1

    Google Chrome

    google.com
    Free

    Save websites into bookmark folders directly from your browser.

    On a computer, open More → Bookmarks and lists → Bookmark manager, then use the manager’s menu to add a new folder. Open the page you want, click the address-bar star, and choose that folder. You can also drag existing bookmarks into it.

    Chrome is the easiest starting point if you already browse there: local bookmarking is free and needs no separate account. Saving bookmarks to your Google Account makes them available across your Chrome devices. Follow Google’s bookmark folder instructions.

    Best for
    Everyday bookmarks in Chrome
    The catch
    Folders need manual sorting, and access across devices requires your Google Account.
    Visit google.com ↗
  2. 2

    Safari

    apple.com
    Free

    Keep links in bookmark folders on your iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

    Safari’s included bookmark folders suit people who mainly use Apple devices. On iPhone, open Bookmarks and find New Folder in the editing controls or More menu; the location varies with your Safari layout. When adding a bookmark, choose its destination folder.

    To move an existing link, touch and hold its bookmark, choose Edit, and change Location. Turn on Safari in iCloud to keep bookmarks available across your Apple devices. Apple documents the controls in its iPhone bookmark guide.

    Best for
    iPhone and Mac users
    The catch
    Safari is for Apple devices; syncing bookmarks requires an Apple Account and Safari enabled in iCloud.
    Visit apple.com ↗
  3. 3

    Firefox

    firefox.com
    Free & open source

    Organize bookmarks into folders and subfolders with desktop controls.

    Firefox is useful when you want a familiar folder tree for a growing reference library. On desktop, open Bookmarks → Manage bookmarks, select the parent location, then use the Organize menu or context menu to add a folder. Drag saved pages into it.

    Nested folders let you separate a broad subject such as Work into individual projects. Local bookmarks work without registration; a Mozilla account adds syncing across Firefox devices. Mozilla’s folder guide explains creating and moving folders.

    Best for
    A manually organized reference library
    The catch
    You organize folders yourself, and syncing requires a Mozilla account.
    Visit firefox.com ↗
  4. 4

    Raindrop.io

    raindrop.io
    Free plan

    Collect links into searchable collections independently of your browser.

    Raindrop.io calls its folders collections. Create one for a project, then choose it when saving a page through the app or browser extension. Tags give you another way to find a link without building a deeper folder tree.

    Its standout free feature is unlimited bookmarks and collections across unlimited devices, with no trial deadline. Choose it if your phone and computer use different browsers. The official free-plan explanation confirms those allowances; advanced features sit outside the free core.

    Best for
    Collections across browsers and devices
    The catch
    An account is required; full-text search and permanent page archives are outside the free plan.
    Visit raindrop.io ↗
  5. 5

    Start.me

    start.me
    Free with ads

    Arrange groups of saved links on a visual start page.

    Start.me suits links you want to see together: work dashboards, course resources, or daily tools. Create a page, add a Bookmarks widget, and group related URLs inside it. These visible groups serve a similar purpose to folders, though the layout feels more like a dashboard.

    The free version allows a limited number of pages, while each bookmark widget can hold unlimited links. Choose it for frequently opened resources rather than a deep folder hierarchy. See its bookmark widget guide and plan comparison.

    Best for
    A visual dashboard of regular links
    The catch
    Registration is required, free pages are limited, and the free version includes ads and tracking.
    Visit start.me ↗
  6. 6

    Google Drive

    workspace.google.com
    Free plan

    Store a document of clickable links inside a project folder.

    Google Drive works when links belong beside documents and other project files. Create a folder, create a Google Doc inside it, and paste your URLs with short descriptions. This is a manual link index, rather than a dedicated bookmark collection.

    The advantage is context: collaborators can read why each source matters alongside the project’s files. Personal accounts include free storage, with a shared allowance across Google services. Google explains creating files in Drive and its storage limits.

    Best for
    Links alongside project documents
    The catch
    A Google Account is required; storage is limited and shared with other Google services, and links need manual entry.
    Visit workspace.google.com ↗

A practical routine for keeping link folders useful

Start with three destinations: Inbox, Active projects, and Reference. Save uncertain links to Inbox, then give each useful bookmark a name that explains its purpose. “Passport renewal checklist” is easier to recognize than a vague page title.

Once a week, empty Inbox and remove links you no longer need. Keep folders broad until a specific project needs its own space. A bookmark stores an address; it does not guarantee that the original page will remain available.

Quick answers

How to save links in Chrome?

Click the address-bar star and choose a folder. Create folders first in Bookmark manager if you want a clear destination for each save.

How to save links on iPhone or in Safari?

Use Safari’s Add Bookmark command and select a folder. To reorganize an existing bookmark, edit its Location. The Safari item above links to Apple’s layout-specific instructions.

How to save links to files or on desktop?

For a plain file, paste URLs into a text document and save it in your chosen desktop folder. For a portable bookmark collection, Chrome’s Bookmark manager offers Export bookmarks, which creates an HTML file. That file stores links, not offline copies of the pages. See Google’s export instructions.

How to save links to Google Drive?

Put a Google Doc containing your URLs inside a Drive folder. You can also upload a text file of links. A Drive shortcut to a Drive file is different from a bookmark to an outside website.
