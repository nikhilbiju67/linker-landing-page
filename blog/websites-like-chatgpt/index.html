10 free websites like ChatGPT and what each does best

By the Linkker team · 18 November 2026 · 6 min read

These free websites like ChatGPT are for anyone who wants help drafting, researching, or working through a problem without paying for ChatGPT Plus. If what you miss is being able to ask follow-up questions, upload a document, or turn a rough idea into a usable draft, several free plans cover those jobs.

Quick picks

  1. Google Gemini: Everyday chat and file questions
  2. Claude: Editing and revising drafts
  3. Perplexity: Web research with sources
  4. Microsoft Copilot: Quick drafts and web lookups
  5. Duck.ai: No-account AI chat
  6. Gemini Notebook: Study notes and source-based PDF questions
  7. DeepSeek: Coding explanations and small debugging questions
  8. Mistral Vibe: Short research and drafting tasks
  9. Poe: Comparing answers across AI models
  10. Grok: Following an announcement and reactions on X
  1. 1

    Google Gemini

    gemini.google.com
    Free plan

    Draft, brainstorm, and ask follow-up questions about files in one chat.

    Gemini is a good first stop if you used ChatGPT for a mix of tasks. Upload a project brief, ask it to pull out the deadlines and open questions, then turn that list into an email in the same thread. The free tier covers everyday chat and file uploads, but large files and long conversations use more of your allowance. If you regularly work through several big documents in one sitting, that’s where the limits matter. Gemini usage limits

    Best for
    Everyday chat and file questions
    The catch
    Large uploads and long chats use more of your free allowance.
    Visit gemini.google.com ↗
  2. 2

    Claude

    claude.com
    Free plan

    Revise drafts and work through longer pieces of writing in chat.

    Claude fits the kind of editing you do in several passes. Paste a draft, ask which claims need more evidence, then have it rewrite the opening without touching the rest. Its Artifacts feature can put a draft in a separate pane so it stays visible while you discuss changes. Free usage has both session and weekly limits, so several rounds on a long document can eat into the time you have for other chats. Claude usage and length limits

    Best for
    Editing and revising drafts
    The catch
    Session and weekly limits can interrupt long editing sessions.
    Visit claude.com ↗
  3. 3

    Perplexity

    perplexity.ai
    Free plan

    Search the web and get a written answer with links to its sources.

    Perplexity replaces the part of ChatGPT you use to look things up. Ask for the difference between two tools, then open the linked documentation to check a claim about what’s included. Standard search is free, while Pro searches are tightly limited. For a comparison you’ll share with someone else, follow the citations on the details that could change your decision. A linked page can be relevant to the topic and still fail to support the specific claim. Perplexity plan details

    Best for
    Web research with sources
    The catch
    The free plan includes very few Pro searches.
    Visit perplexity.ai ↗
  4. 4

    Microsoft Copilot

    copilot.com
    Free plan

    Draft emails, brainstorm, and get answers informed by web search.

    The free consumer version of Copilot handles the jobs you might open ChatGPT for between meetings. Turn scattered notes into a short recap, draft a polite follow-up, or look up a product detail and use it in the reply. It’s a practical fit if you already sign in with a Microsoft account. Budget for a little copying and pasting if you work in Word or Outlook, since the deeper in-app features depend on an eligible Microsoft 365 plan. Microsoft’s Copilot comparison

    Best for
    Quick drafts and web lookups
    The catch
    Deep integration in desktop Office apps requires an eligible Microsoft 365 plan.
    Visit copilot.com ↗
  5. 5

    Duck.ai

    duck.ai
    Free plan

    Chat with a choice of AI models without creating an account.

    Duck.ai is handy when you want a quick rewrite or a plain-English explanation and would rather skip sign-up. You can choose from several free models, which is useful if the first answer is too vague or too wordy. DuckDuckGo anonymizes chats before sending them to model providers. There’s a daily limit, and the model you choose affects how quickly you reach it, so it’s a better fit for occasional questions than a full afternoon of back-and-forth. Duck.ai access and privacy · Daily limits

    Best for
    No-account AI chat
    The catch
    Daily usage is capped, and some models use the allowance faster.
    Visit duck.ai ↗
  6. 6

    Gemini Notebook

    notebook.google
    Free plan

    Ask questions about a set of documents and jump to the passages behind each answer.

    Gemini Notebook, formerly NotebookLM, replaces ChatGPT’s document Q&A when the source material matters more than open-ended chat. Add lecture slides, readings, and your own notes, then ask for an explanation with references you can click to check. It’s especially useful when you need to see where two readings disagree. You need a Google account and a set of sources to start, and the free tier limits chat usage and how many sources you can add. Google’s Gemini Notebook limits · How source citations work

    Best for
    Study notes and source-based PDF questions
    The catch
    Requires a Google account and caps both chat usage and sources per notebook.
    Visit notebook.google ↗
  7. 7

    DeepSeek

    deepseek.com
    Free

    Work through coding questions and step-by-step explanations in free chat.

    DeepSeek is an option for the coding questions you’d normally paste into ChatGPT. Give it a small function and a confusing error, then ask it to trace the inputs and explain where the result goes wrong. The consumer chat is free, and the official homepage links directly to it. You’ll need an account, and the service’s privacy policy says it collects chat inputs, so a small example with fake data is a better fit than a dump of private logs. DeepSeek’s free chat offering · DeepSeek privacy policy

    Best for
    Coding explanations and small debugging questions
    The catch
    Requires an account, and DeepSeek collects what you submit in chat.
    Visit deepseek.com ↗
  8. 8

    Mistral Vibe

    mistral.ai
    Free plan

    Draft and summarize in chat, with limited web searches on the free plan.

    Mistral Vibe, formerly Le Chat, suits short tasks that mix writing and lookup. Ask it to find the official instructions for a task, then turn those instructions into a checklist you can follow. Its chat has a fast mode for short answers and a thinking mode for more involved requests. The free plan limits both messages and web searches, which matters if your first pass turns into a research session with a long string of follow-ups. Mistral’s Vibe documentation · Mistral plan comparison

    Best for
    Short research and drafting tasks
    The catch
    Free messages and web searches are both limited.
    Visit mistral.ai ↗
  9. 9

    Poe

    poe.com
    Free plan

    Compare answers from different AI bots under one account.

    Poe is useful when you want a second take on the same prompt. Give two bots the same paragraph and compare what each one cuts, or ask both to explain a tricky concept and keep the clearer example. Free users get a daily pool of points shared across bots, and different bots can use different amounts. That setup works for quick comparisons, but an expensive bot or a long conversation can burn through the pool fast. Poe’s guide to points

    Best for
    Comparing answers across AI models
    The catch
    Bots share a daily points budget, and some use it much faster than others.
    Visit poe.com ↗
  10. 10

    Grok

    x.ai
    Free plan

    Ask about a developing story and search the web and public posts on X.

    Grok is most useful when the conversation on X is part of what you’re trying to understand. If a company announces a feature, ask for the original announcement and the main questions people raised afterward. Its free plan covers limited chat and search. Open the original announcement for facts such as rollout dates, and read the posts themselves if you need to know who said what. A summary can blur the difference between a firsthand report and somebody repeating it. Grok’s search and access FAQ · Grok plan comparison

    Best for
    Following an announcement and reactions on X
    The catch
    Free chat requests are limited, and access can vary by location.
    Visit x.ai ↗

Quick answers

What is the best free alternative to ChatGPT?

Start with Google Gemini if you want one chat for everyday writing, brainstorming, and questions about files. Pick Claude if most of your time goes into revising prose, or Perplexity if you usually need links you can open and verify. Try the same real task in two tools and see which gives you a better first answer and handles your follow-up with fewer corrections.

Is there an alternative to ChatGPT without signing up?

Duck.ai lets you start a free chat and choose a model without creating an account. It suits quick tasks like shortening an email or explaining a spreadsheet formula. The daily cap matters more if you plan to use it for repeated follow-ups. Duck.ai access details

Which free ChatGPT alternative is best for research papers?

Use Perplexity to find sources on the open web and Gemini Notebook to ask questions about PDFs you already have. For a literature review, use free websites for finding research papers to track down the original publications, then check the paper itself before citing a finding.
