10 free AI tools for teachers, organized by your teaching week

By the Linkker team · 15 November 2026 · 7 min read

These free AI tools for teachers help busy educators plan lessons, adapt materials, and prepare classroom activities without starting a paid subscription. The list follows the work of a teaching week: plan first, prepare resources, check understanding, then tackle the tasks left on your desk.

Every pick has an ongoing free option for the job described, though accounts, generation caps, and restricted features are common. You only need two or three to start. Treat generated materials as drafts: check facts, answer keys, reading difficulty, and alignment with what you actually teach before handing anything out.

Quick picks

  1. MagicSchool: Planning the week’s lessons
  2. Khanmigo: Planning around Khan Academy resources
  3. Eduaide: Building supporting lesson materials
  4. Diffit: Preparing differentiated reading materials
  5. Brisk Teaching: Creating activities from existing sources
  6. Canva: Turning lesson drafts into visual materials
  7. QuestionWell: Writing comprehension checks
  8. Curipod: A recurring live writing activity
  9. SchoolAI: Drafting and revising teacher documents
  10. Goblin Tools: Admin tasks and project checklists

    MagicSchool

    Free plan

    Draft lesson plans, rubrics, and exit tickets through tools built around teaching tasks.

    Start here when Monday’s lesson still needs a structure. MagicSchool gives you separate tools for familiar classroom jobs, so you can choose a lesson planner or rubric generator instead of constructing a long chatbot prompt. Specify the grade, objective, and available time, then edit the result around your students’ needs. The ongoing free plan covers core classroom tools, with generation limits shown inside your account. Free-plan details.

    eduaide.ai

Set it up in 10 minutes

Build around one lesson you already need to teach. Ten minutes is enough to prepare a useful draft; account verification may take longer.

  1. Minutes 0–2: Choose one planning tool, such as MagicSchool or Khanmigo. Enter the grade, learning objective, lesson length, and materials available.
  2. Minutes 2–5: Generate one resource. Pick Eduaide for an organizer, Diffit for reading support, or Brisk for an activity based on an existing source.
  3. Minutes 5–7: Draft a short comprehension check in QuestionWell. Read every question and verify the answer key against the source.
  4. Minutes 7–9: Edit the wording, check factual claims, and remove activities that do not fit your class or available time.
  5. Minute 9–10: Save the finished resource and its source links together. These ways to save links in a folder help keep next week’s preparation easy to find.

Keep student names, grades, and private records out of prompts unless your school has approved that specific use.

Quick answers

What are the best AI tools for teachers?

For this starter kit, choose by task: MagicSchool for planning, Diffit for reading support, QuestionWell for comprehension questions, and Curipod for a recurring live writing activity. Start with the job that consumes the most preparation time.

Are these actually free tools for teachers?

Yes, for the core jobs described. Some include introductory premium access, but an ongoing free option remains. Check the catch before depending on exports, classroom sessions, or advanced features.

Which useful websites for teachers should I bookmark first?

Bookmark one planner, one resource creator, and one assessment tool. Three sites you understand are more useful than a dozen accounts you rarely open.

Can AI tools grade student work automatically?

Some offer grading or feedback features, but this starter kit works best for preparation and formative checks. Review feedback yourself before it reaches students, especially when it could affect a grade.
